## How to Choose: Three Questions, Not Twenty

**1. What battery are you willing to carry?** This decides the light's size more than anything else. An 18650 cell means a light about the length of a marker pen and real runtime. A 14500 (AA-sized lithium) halves the bulk and roughly halves the runtime. There is no third option worth having at this price.

**2. Do you want a menu or a switch?** Anduril 2 firmware — standard on Sofirn and Wurkkos — gives you ramping, strobe, lockout, battery check, and a genuinely deep configuration tree. Enthusiasts love it. If the phrase "hold from off for 3 clicks" makes you tired, buy a two-mode light instead and never think about it again.

**3. How often will you actually charge it?** Onboard USB-C is the single most useful feature at this price. A light you top up from the same cable as your phone gets carried; one that needs a separate cell charger ends up in a drawer. See our [USB-C flashlight guide](/usb-c-rechargeable-flashlights_-the-complete-2025-buyers-guide/) for why.

## 1. Sofirn SC31 Pro — Best Overall Under $50

This is the light the enthusiast forums have defaulted to for years, and the reason is unglamorous: it does everything competently and costs almost nothing. The SST40 emitter pushes **2,000 lumens** with about **226 m of throw**, the 18650 cell charges over **USB-C right in the light**, it's rated **IPX8**, and it runs full Anduril 2.

The runtime numbers are what actually matter for carry. Turbo is a party trick that thermally steps down within minutes; the useful figures are **900 lumens for roughly 2 hours 10 minutes** on High and **300 lumens for about 5 hours 50 minutes** on Medium. That medium mode is more light than most people ever need and it lasts an evening — the same logic we apply in the [power outage kit guide](/best-flashlights-for-power-outages/).

The cost is bulk. An 18650 tube at ~4.6 inches rides fine in a jacket or bag, less happily in jeans. And Anduril will confuse anyone who doesn't want to learn it. [Check the Sofirn SC31 Pro →](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=sofirn+sc31+pro&tag=lightertorch-20)

## 2. Wurkkos TS10 V2 — Best If You Want to Forget It's There

**32 grams without a cell**, 71.5 mm long, 21 mm head. This is a light that disappears into a coin pocket, and it still does **1,400 lumens** from three CSP emitters — with **90 CRI**, which is the spec nobody advertises and everybody notices. High-CRI light renders colour honestly: skin, wiring insulation, wood grain, and blood all look like themselves rather than washed-out grey-blue. Once you've carried a 90 CRI light, a cheap cool-white one looks broken. It's **IP68**, runs Anduril 2, and adds RGB aux LEDs under the optics so you can find it in the dark.

Two honest warnings. First, **it does not run on AA batteries** despite being AA-sized — it needs a 14500 lithium cell, and feeding it an alkaline AA will not work properly. Second, a 14500 in a light this small means turbo is brief; treat the high modes as bursts, not settings.

Pick the 4000K version if you're choosing. Neutral white is easier on the eyes and better outdoors. [Check the Wurkkos TS10 V2 →](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=wurkkos+ts10+v2&tag=lightertorch-20)

## 3. Olight i5R EOS — Best for People Who Hate Flashlight Menus

Some people want a flashlight, not a hobby. The i5R EOS is a **350-lumen**, two-mode, tail-switch light in an AA-sized body with the 14500 cell and **USB-C charging built in**. Click for high, half-press or click again for low. That's the entire manual.

Its numbers look modest next to the others and that's mostly framing: 350 lumens with a **64 m throw** covers a driveway, a crawlspace, a dog walk, and a dropped key — the real jobs. What sells it is the low mode: **15 lumens for 37 hours**, which means it is genuinely still working two nights into an outage while the turbo-chasing lights are dead. High runs 350 lumens for about 10 minutes then holds 150 lumens for roughly another 170 minutes.

At 2.12 oz and 95.4 mm it's a comfortable pocket carry. Olight's proprietary charging on other models annoys people; this one is straightforward. [Check the Olight i5R EOS →](https://www.amazon.com/OLIGHT-Rechargeable-Tail-Switch-Flashlight-Emergency/dp/B09FZ19628/?tag=lightertorch-20)

## What to Skip in This Price Band

- **"100,000 lumen" listings.** No pocket light makes five figures of lumens. Inflated marketing claims are the single most reliable sign of a light whose other specs are also fiction — more red flags in [what to look for in a good LED flashlight](/things-to-look-for-in-a-good-led-flashlights/).
- **Lights with a sealed, unnamed battery.** When the cell dies in two years the whole light is landfill.
- **Zoomable "tactical" kits** with a plastic bezel and a bundled charger. The zoom mechanism kills the beam and breaks first.
- **Anything without a stated water rating.** IPX4 is a real answer; silence is not.

## FAQ

**How many lumens do I need for everyday carry?** Far fewer than the box art suggests. 200–400 lumens handles almost every real EDC task; 1,000+ is for reassurance and the occasional outdoor sweep. Runtime at *usable* output matters more than peak — see our [lumens guide](/how-many-lumens-should-flashlight-have-2/).

**Is 18650 or 14500 better for EDC?** 18650 for runtime and output, 14500 for carry comfort. If the light lives in a bag, take 18650. If it lives in your pocket, take 14500 — the light you actually carry beats the brighter one you leave at home.

**What is high CRI and should I pay for it?** CRI measures how accurately a light renders colour, on a 100-point scale. 90+ looks natural; typical budget emitters sit around 70 and make everything look flat and blue. It costs a small amount of peak brightness. For anyone inspecting, repairing, or photographing anything, it's the best upgrade at this price.

**Do I need Anduril?** No. It's powerful and free, but a two-mode light with a good low mode covers 95% of use. Buy the interface you'll enjoy, not the one with the longest feature list.

**Is it worth spending more than $50?** Only for specific things: serious throw, certified impact and submersion ratings, or a warranty you'll actually use. Capability-per-dollar peaks right about here — the full argument is in [$20 vs $100 flashlight](/20-vs-100-dollar-flashlight/).

**What if I won't carry something this size?** Then buy smaller rather than buying nothing — a light on your keys beats a better light in a drawer.

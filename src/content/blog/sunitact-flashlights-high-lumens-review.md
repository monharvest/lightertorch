**Quick answer:** The [Sunitact ST1476](https://www.amazon.com/Flashlights-Sunitact-Rechargeable-Emergency-Waterproof/dp/B09PDVKSVD/?tag=lightertorch-20) is a cheap zoomable Amazon flashlight with USB-C charging and a power-bank port. It is **not** a 990,000-lumen light, and the current **20,000-lumen** listing is still not an ANSI measurement. The LED Sunitact names — a Cree XHP70.2 — has a published ceiling of about **4,100 lumens**. Buy this only as a drawer/car light. If you actually need a flashlight, spend the same money on an honest budget light instead.

## What Sunitact is actually selling

Checked on Amazon on **22 August 2026**. ASIN `B09PDVKSVD`, sold by **Sunitact-US**, model **ST1476**, Prime price about **$22**.

The listing now says:

- 30 W output and **20,000 lumens**

- **5,000 ft** beam

- USB-C charge in, USB-A power-bank out

Older titles on this same ASIN said **990,000 lumens**. That number is still what a lot of people type when they search “sunitact flashlight.” The seller walked the number down. They did not start measuring it.

## Why the lumen number cannot be true

We have not run this light on an integrating sphere. We do not need to, because the claim already contradicts the LED they named.

[Cree’s own XHP70.2 table](https://www.cree-led.com/products/leds/xlamp/xhp/) lists a **maximum of 4,124 lumens** at about 29 W. That is the chip on a lab board, not a $22 flashlight after heat, optics, and a cheap driver. A 20,000-lumen claim is roughly **5×** that ceiling. A 990,000-lumen claim is stadium-floodlight territory.

|---|---|---|
| Sunitact ST1476 | 20,000 lm (was 990,000) | Marketing copy, not ANSI FL1 |

| [Olight Javelot Turbo](/what-is-the-best-long-range-flashlight/) | 1,300 lm / 1,300 m | A real thrower; still nowhere near 20,000 lm |

The 5,000 ft beam is the same problem. That is about **1,500 m**. Our [long-range guide](/what-is-the-best-long-range-flashlight/) treats 1,300 m as a dedicated thrower with a deep reflector. A zoomable $22 host does not do that.

## What the light *is* good for

Strip the box art and you still have a usable cheap kit:

- USB-C charging and a four-dot battery gauge
- a USB-A out port if your phone is dead
- high / medium / low plus strobe and SOS
- a holster and an AAA carrier in the box
- a price that makes it disposable

That is a glove-box or junk-drawer light. It is not a camping, duty, or “one flashlight for everything” light. Zoom heads also leak, wear, and fight any IP67 claim — the same reason enthusiasts avoid them. We broke that pattern down in [$20 vs $100 flashlight](/20-vs-100-dollar-flashlight/).

## FAQ

**Is the Sunitact flashlight 990,000 lumens?** No. That was an older Amazon title on this same product. The LED they name cannot do it.

**Is 20,000 lumens real?** Not as an ANSI FL1 rating. Cree publishes about 4,100 lumens as the XHP70.2 maximum. Treat 20,000 as advertising.

**Is Sunitact a good flashlight brand?** It is an Amazon seller brand. There is no ANSI sheet, no long warranty culture, and the headline number has already changed once. That is the review.

**Can I use it in the rain?** The listing says IP67. Zoom heads are a weak point for seals. We have not submerged this sample, so do not treat the rating as verified.

**What should I search instead of “sunitact flashlight”?** “ANSI flashlight,” a named brand (Fenix, Streamlight, Sofirn), or our [buying checklist](/things-to-look-for-in-a-good-led-flashlights/).

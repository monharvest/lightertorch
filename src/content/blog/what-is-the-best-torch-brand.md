**Quick answer:** No single brand is best at everything, but four cover almost every need. **Fenix** is the best all-rounder (start with the [PD36R V2.0](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=fenix+pd36r&tag=lightertorch-20)). **Streamlight** is the best value for work and duty (the [Stinger 2020](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=streamlight+stinger+2020&tag=lightertorch-20)). **SureFire** is the premium tactical pick (the [EDCL2-T](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=surefire+edcl2-t&tag=lightertorch-20)). On a tight budget, **Wurkkos** (the [FC11C](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=wurkkos+fc11c&tag=lightertorch-20)) is the cheap brand worth trusting.

Search Amazon for "flashlight" and you'll see hundreds of brand names. The nine below have a track record, publish full specs and put a warranty in writing. We compared them on published specs and each maker's own warranty terms, not on reputation alone. If you're already down to Olight, Fenix or Streamlight, our [Olight vs Fenix vs Streamlight comparison](/olight-vs-fenix-vs-streamlight/) goes model by model.

## 1. Fenix: Best All-Round Flashlight Brand

**Founded:** [2001, Shenzhen, China](https://www.fenixlight.com/company-profile/) · **Best for:** camping, hiking, power cuts, one light for everything

Fenix makes sensible, well-sealed lights on standard rechargeable cells and publishes full runtime figures. The [PD36R V2.0](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=fenix+pd36r&tag=lightertorch-20) is the template: [1,700 lumens, a 396 m beam, IP68 sealing, 18 W USB-C charging and a standard 21700 battery](https://www.fenixlighting.com/products/fenix-pd36r-rechargeable-flashlight), for $99.95 at Fenix's US store.

**Warranty:** Fenix gives [15-day replacement and five years of free repair](https://www.fenixlight.com/service-policy/) (two years for lights with a built-in battery, one year on batteries and chargers), then repairs for life at the cost of parts, with no labor charge. Fenix Lighting, the US distributor, sells this as a limited lifetime warranty.

**Weak spots:** rarely the cheapest option, and the designs are plain.

## 2. Streamlight: Best for Work, Duty and Value

**Founded:** [1973, Eagleville, Pennsylvania](https://www.streamlight.com/community/blog/streamlight-marks-50-years-of-manufacturing-high-quality-lighting) · **Best for:** police, trades, vehicles, beater lights

Streamlight is the most common brand on US police belts (more in [what flashlights police use](/what-flashlights-do-police-use/)) and is often cheaper than Fenix or Olight at similar output. The [Stinger 2020](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=streamlight+stinger+2020&tag=lightertorch-20) is a good example: [2,000 lumens, a 315 m beam and 2 hours on high](https://www.streamlight.com/products/detail/stinger-2020). The polymer-bodied [PolyTac](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=streamlight+polytac&tag=lightertorch-20) is the cheap, tough option: [600 lumens, IPX7 and impact-tested to 3 m](https://www.streamlight.com/products/detail/polytac).

**Warranty:** [limited lifetime](https://www.streamlight.com/support/service/warranty-information), except that rechargeable batteries, chargers, switches and electronics get two years. A few lines, including the Sidewinder, KeyMate USB, Pocket Mate, QB and Bandit, aren't covered by the lifetime terms at all.

**Weak spots:** plainer designs, and some rechargeables use Streamlight's own battery packs instead of standard cells.

## 3. Olight: Best for Everyday Carry

**Founded:** 2007, Shenzhen, China · **Best for:** pocket carry, gifts, people who charge rather than swap batteries

Olight's strengths are small, dense bodies and magnetic charging: the MCC cable snaps onto the tail cap, with no port to open. The [Baton 4 Pro](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=olight+baton+4+pro&tag=lightertorch-20) is the benchmark pocket light, and the [Warrior series](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=olight+warrior+tactical&tag=lightertorch-20) covers tactical use.

**Warranty:** for lights bought in the US after January 1, 2023, Olight offers a [lifetime warranty](https://support.olight.com/4f43/ea78/cba5) on lights and knives, with two years on rechargeable batteries and one year on the MCC cable.

**Weak spots:** many models use Olight's own customized cells, so a spare 18650 from the drawer won't necessarily fit, and on magnetic-only models a lost cable means buying another Olight one.

## 4. SureFire: Best Premium Tactical Flashlight Brand

**Founded:** [1979, California](https://www.surefire.com/surefire-story/) · **Best for:** tactical, duty and hard-use lights where price is secondary

SureFire began with laser sights in 1979 and introduced the 6C tactical flashlight in 1988, followed soon after by the 6P. Both ran on 123A lithium cells, the format many SureFire lights still use. The [EDCL2-T](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=surefire+edcl2-t&tag=lightertorch-20), which SureFire calls its most capable everyday-carry light, is typical: [1,200 lumens, 210 m, 1 hour on high, two 123A cells, IPX7, $265](https://www.surefire.com/edcl2-t/).

**Warranty:** [lifetime on the light](https://www.surefire.com/policies/warranty/); rechargeable batteries and chargers get two years.

**Weak spots:** price. The EDCL2-T costs over twice as much as the Fenix PD36R V2.0 for fewer lumens; you pay for the switch, the build and the reputation. Many models still run on disposable 123A cells.

## 5. Nitecore: Best for Slim, High-Output EDC Lights

**Founded:** [2007](https://www.nitecore.us/aboutus) · **Best for:** flat pocket lights, tactical lights, gadget fans

Nitecore packs a lot of output into thin bodies. The [EDC37](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=nitecore+edc37&tag=lightertorch-20) is rated at [8,000 lumens from a built-in 8,000 mAh battery](https://www.nitecore.us/product/edc37), although that figure is a floodlight burst; its 420 m throw comes from a separate 3,000-lumen search mode. The more conventional [P20iX](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=nitecore+p20ix&tag=lightertorch-20) tactical light is rated at [4,000 lumens and 221 m on a 21700 cell](https://www.nitecore.us/product/p20ix).

**Warranty:** [60 months on lights with removable batteries and 24 months on lights with built-in batteries](https://www.nitecore.us/article/416), then a limited lifetime warranty that covers labor but not parts. Claims go through the authorized dealer you bought from.

**Weak spots:** the EDC series uses built-in batteries, so you can't swap a cell and the warranty is shorter. Nitecore's own FAQ notes that the EDC37 turns itself down during long high-output use to manage heat.

## 6. Acebeam: Best for the Brightest Flashlights

**Founded:** [2014 (factory since 2009), Shenzhen, China](https://www.acebeam.com/aboutus) · **Best for:** maximum output, long throw, long warranty

Acebeam builds some of the brightest lights on sale, including the fan-cooled [X75](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=acebeam+x75&tag=lightertorch-20), rated at [80,000 lumens and listed at $419.90](https://www.acebeam.com/x75). Its everyday [E75](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=acebeam+e75&tag=lightertorch-20) is more practical: [up to 4,500 lumens on one 21700 cell, USB-C charging and IP68 sealing for $99.90](https://www.acebeam.com/e75), and Acebeam states plainly how fast its turbo steps down (more on that below).

**Warranty:** in May 2025 Acebeam [extended free repair from five years to ten](https://www.acebeam.com/acebeam-unveils-exciting-warranty-extension-from-5-years-to-10-years), including lights bought before the change. After [ten years, repairs cost parts and shipping only](https://www.acebeam.com/acebeamwarranty).

**Weak spots:** the headline lights are big, heavy and expensive, and warranty claims start with the dealer you bought from.

## 7. Maglite: Best Simple Flashlight on Everyday Batteries

**Founded:** [1955, Los Angeles area](https://maglite.com/blogs/maghistory-blog-1/company-history) · **Best for:** glovebox and household lights, anyone who wants AA or D batteries

Maglite lights are [designed and assembled at the company's only factory, in Southern California](https://maglite.com/blogs/maghistory-blog-1/what-it-means-to-be-a-u-s-a-manufacturer), though some parts are imported. The LED models are far better than the old incandescent ones. The [ML300LX 2D](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=maglite+ml300lx&tag=lightertorch-20) gives [524 lumens and a 366 m beam on two D cells, with up to 69 hours in Eco mode, for $44.99](https://maglite.com/products/maglite-ml300lx-led-2d-flashlight), and the [Mini Maglite Pro](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=mini+maglite+pro&tag=lightertorch-20) gets [332 lumens from two AAs for $24.99](https://maglite.com/products/mini-maglite-pro-led-pocket-purse-flashlight-1).

**Warranty:** Maglite's current warranty page lists a [ten-year limited warranty](https://maglite.com/pages/warranty), not the lifetime cover the brand is often remembered for, and it excludes LED burnout, battery leakage and battery exhaustion.

**Weak spots:** heavy for the output, and only IPX4 (splash-resistant) where most brands here rate their lights IPX7 or IP68. Our [durable flashlights guide](/the-most-durable-flashlights-for-outdoor-use-a-comprehensive-guide/) explains what those ratings mean in practice.

## 8. Wurkkos: Best Budget Flashlight Brand

**Founded:** [2019, Shenzhen, China](https://wurkkos.com/pages/about-wurkkos) · **Best for:** enthusiasts and anyone on a budget

Wurkkos is the value pick of the enthusiast world: high-CRI LEDs, magnetic tail caps, USB-C and the open-source Anduril interface at low prices. The [FC11C](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=wurkkos+fc11c&tag=lightertorch-20) is a [1,200-lumen 18650 light with a high-CRI Nichia 519A LED, USB-C charging and a magnetic tail](https://wurkkos.com/products/wurkkos-fc11c-nichia-519a-buck-circuit-flashlight). The [TS30S Pro](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=wurkkos+ts30s+pro&tag=lightertorch-20) is rated at [6,000 lumens and 1,086 m](https://wurkkos.com/products/ts30s-pro) for well under $100. [Sofirn](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=sofirn+flashlight&tag=lightertorch-20) is a similar budget brand worth comparing.

**Warranty:** [one year](https://wurkkos.com/pages/faq). You pay shipping to Wurkkos, and [the repair itself is free](https://wurkkos.com/pages/return-refund-policy).

**Weak spots:** a short warranty handled by email, and Anduril's click-heavy interface is more than many people want.

## 9. Klarus: Best for Dual-Switch Tactical Lights

The bigger [XT21X Pro](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=klarus+xt21x+pro&tag=lightertorch-20) is rated at [4,400 lumens on a 5,000 mAh cell](https://www.klaruslight.com/products/xt21x-pro-flagship-tactical-flashlight.html). You can [browse the full Klarus range](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=klarus+flashlight&tag=lightertorch-20) on Amazon.

## Which Brands Make the Brightest Flashlights?

If raw output is the goal, three brands lead:

- **Imalent.** The [MS18](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=imalent+ms18&tag=lightertorch-20) is rated at 100,000 lumens. [1lumen measured about 101,600 lumens at switch-on and about 69,900 lumens 30 seconds later](https://1lumen.com/review/imalent-ms18/); it takes 18 LEDs, eight 21700 cells and two cooling fans to get there.
- **Acebeam.** The X75 is rated at 80,000 lumens and [steps down to 30,000 lumens when it gets too hot](https://www.acebeam.com/x75).
- **Nitecore.** The EDC37 claims 8,000 lumens from a pocket light, as a short floodlight burst.

For most people, peak lumens is the wrong number to shop on. Sustained output and beam distance matter more, as we explain in [lumens vs candela vs beam distance](/lumens-vs-candela-vs-beam-distance/).

## How to Judge a Flashlight Brand

**ANSI FL1 ratings.** Established brands rate their lights to the ANSI/PLATO FL1 standard. Beam distance is where the light falls to 0.25 lux, and runtime is [the time until output drops to 10% of the level measured 30 seconds after switch-on](https://www.streamlight.com/resources/learning/ansi-information). That runtime rule matters: a light can fall from turbo to a fraction of it and still quote a long runtime. Look for runtime tables or graphs, not a single figure.

**Honest step-down specs.** Good brands say how long the peak lasts. Acebeam lists the E75's turbo as 4,500 falling to 1,500 lumens after a minute, and says the X75 can hold 20,000 lumens for 20 minutes. A brand that lists only the peak is hiding the part you'll actually use.

**Standard vs proprietary batteries.** 18650, 21700, AA and D cells are sold everywhere and can be swapped in the field. Proprietary cells, battery packs and built-in batteries tie you to one maker, and built-in batteries usually shorten the warranty: Nitecore gives 24 months instead of 60, Fenix two years instead of five.

**USB-C charging.** On a rechargeable light, a USB-C port means any phone cable will do. Magnetic cables are convenient until you lose one. More in our [USB-C rechargeable flashlight guide](/usb-c-rechargeable-flashlights_-the-complete-2025-buyers-guide/).

**Warranty and US support.** Read the exclusions and who does the repair. Streamlight's lifetime cover excludes batteries and limits electronics to two years. Olight's lifetime cover applies to US purchases made after January 2023. Fenix Lighting US [refers Amazon buyers to the seller's own service center](https://www.fenixlighting.com/pages/warranty-repairs) rather than handling the claim itself, and Nitecore and Acebeam route claims through the dealer you bought from. Keep your receipt either way.

## Red Flags of No-Name Amazon Flashlight Brands

- **Impossible lumen claims.** If a fan-cooled searchlight with 18 LEDs and eight 21700 cells tops out around 100,000 lumens, a pocket zoom light claiming 90,000 lumens is fiction.
- **Impossible battery claims.** Reputable brands ship 18650 cells of around 3,000 mAh (Klarus's own cell is rated 3,100 mAh, and Wurkkos bundles 3,000 mAh cells). A "9,900 mAh 18650" is not a real rating.
- **Missing specs.** No runtime table, no candela or beam-distance figure, no IP rating. Brands that follow FL1 publish all of them.
- **No warranty page or service address.** Every brand in this guide publishes one. If the warranty is "contact seller" and the brand has no website, Amazon's return window is your real warranty.
- **Rotating brand names.** The same light and the same photos sold under a different string of capital letters on each listing.

Cheap is fine; anonymous isn't. A Wurkkos or Sofirn light costs about the same as a no-name one and comes with real specs and a real warranty. Our [best EDC flashlights under $50](/best-edc-flashlights-under-50/) covers the best of them.

## FAQ

**What is the best flashlight brand?** For most people, Fenix. Its lights, such as the PD36R V2.0, run on standard rechargeable cells, charge over USB-C, are sealed to IP68 and come with five years of free repair. Streamlight is the better value for work and duty use, SureFire is the premium tactical choice, and Wurkkos is the best budget brand.

**What is the best torch brand in the UK?** The same brands lead in Britain, where "torch" is the usual word. Ledlenser, [founded in Germany in 1994](https://www.ledlenserusa.com/pages/our-history), is also worth a look, and its P7 is a common UK police issue light. Buy from an authorized UK dealer, since several brands only honor warranties through their own distributors.

**Is Olight better than Fenix?** Neither is better overall. Olight makes smaller, more polished pocket lights with magnetic charging and, for US buyers, a lifetime warranty. Fenix rechargeables such as the PD36R V2.0 use standard 21700 cells and USB-C, which suits camping and emergencies better. Our [Olight vs Fenix vs Streamlight comparison](/olight-vs-fenix-vs-streamlight/) goes model by model.

**Are cheap Amazon flashlight brands worth it?** Established budget brands are. Wurkkos and Sofirn sell well-specified lights for $20–$60, and Wurkkos puts a one-year warranty in writing. Anonymous brands with six-figure lumen claims usually aren't: the specs are invented and there's rarely any warranty beyond Amazon's return window.

**Which flashlight brands have a lifetime warranty?** SureFire and Olight (on US purchases since January 2023) cover the light itself for life, and Streamlight offers a limited lifetime warranty with two years on rechargeable batteries, chargers, switches and electronics. Fenix, Nitecore and Acebeam give five to ten years of free repair, then repair for life where you pay for parts. Maglite now lists ten years and Wurkkos one.

**What flashlight brand do police use?** In the US, mostly Streamlight, with SureFire second. In the UK, the Ledlenser P7 is the common issue light. Duty lights typically run 800–1,200 lumens; see [what flashlights police use](/what-flashlights-do-police-use/) for models and civilian equivalents.

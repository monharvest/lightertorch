**Quick answer:** A lighter does two jobs at once: it lets fuel out, and it makes a spark to light it. In a disposable lighter, rolling the thumb wheel scrapes a ferrocerium "flint" to throw sparks, while holding the lever opens a valve that releases gas from the pressurized tank. A push-button lighter swaps the flint for a piezo crystal that makes a high-voltage spark. A Zippo wicks up liquid fuel instead of releasing gas, and an arc lighter uses a battery and no fuel at all.

Once you know which of those designs you're holding, most lighter problems (and most "can I fix it?" questions) answer themselves.

## Parts of a Lighter

This is a standard disposable flint lighter, the design most people picture. Hold a clear-bodied one up to the light and you can see most of these parts.

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Fuel tank** | Holds the fuel as a liquid under pressure. BIC's pocket lighters use [essentially pure isobutane](https://nam.bic.com/en-us/lighter-frequently-asked-questions). |
| **Valve** | A spring-loaded seal at the top of the tank. Closed unless the lever is lifting it. |
| **Lever** (gas button) | The part your thumb presses after the wheel. It lifts the valve so gas flows, and lets it snap shut when you let go. |
| **Burner nozzle** | Where the gas comes out and burns. On a BIC the flame height is [fixed and can't be adjusted](https://nam.bic.com/en-us/lighter-frequently-asked-questions); refillables usually have an adjuster wheel. |
| **Spark wheel** | A small steel wheel with a rough, grooved edge. Rolling it scrapes the flint. |
| **Flint** | A tiny rod of ferrocerium pressed up against the wheel. It's the part that actually makes the sparks. |
| **Flint spring** | Keeps pushing the flint up against the wheel as it wears down. |
| **Wind guard** | The metal hood around the nozzle. It shelters the flame and holds the wheel in place. |
| **Child guard** | A stiff metal band or mechanism that makes the wheel or button harder to operate. Required on disposable lighters in the US since 1994. |

## How a Disposable Lighter Makes a Flame

1. **You roll the wheel.** Its rough edge scrapes tiny particles off the flint.
2. **The particles catch fire in the air.** "Flint" is really [ferrocerium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrocerium), an alloy of iron and rare-earth metals in which cerium is the main ingredient. Freshly scraped cerium ignites at only 150–180 °C, so the shavings burn as sparks the moment they're torn off.
3. **You're already pressing the lever.** It lifts the valve, and the liquid fuel boils into gas as it leaves the pressurized tank. Isobutane boils at about −12 °C (11 °F) ([PubChem](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/Isobutane)), so at room temperature it turns to gas instantly.
4. **The sparks light the gas** at the nozzle, and the flame keeps burning for as long as the valve stays open.
5. **You let go.** The spring shuts the valve, the gas stops and the flame goes out.

That dependence on the fuel boiling is why lighters struggle in the cold. Near freezing, butane barely vaporizes, so there's little gas to light. More on that in [how to fix a lighter that won't light](/fixing-a-lighter/).

### Why the child guard is there

Since July 12, 1994, every disposable and novelty lighter sold in the US has had to be child-resistant under a federal safety standard. The test is concrete: [at least 85% of a panel of children under five](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-II/subchapter-B/part-1210) must fail to operate it. That's why a new disposable's wheel feels stiff. The guard is doing its job; it isn't a fault.

## How a Push-Button (Piezo) Lighter Works

Push-button lighters and most torch lighters use no flint. Pressing the button compresses a spring until a small hammer snaps free and strikes a crystal, usually lead zirconate titanate (PZT). Squeezing the crystal that suddenly produces a [very high voltage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piezo_ignition), which jumps as a spark between an electrode and the burner. The same press opens the gas valve, so the spark and the gas arrive together.

The parts of a piezo igniter:

- **Button** — what you press. It loads the spring.
- **Hammer and springs** — store your thumb's force and release it all at once. That's the click you feel.
- **Piezo crystal** — turns the hammer blow into a voltage pulse. It isn't a battery and never needs charging; it just wears out, but only after several tens of thousands of strikes.
- **Electrode wire** — carries the pulse to the tip.
- **Spark gap** — the few millimetres of air the spark jumps, right where the gas comes out.

The voltage is higher than most people expect, well over 10,000 volts, as we explain in [how many volts is a gas lighter spark](/voltage-in-a-gas-lighter/). It carries almost no energy, which is why it only stings if it [shocks you](/lighter-cause-a-shock/).

### The kitchen "gas lighter"

The long, fuel-free spark lighter many kitchens keep for the stove is the same piezo igniter in a longer body. It has no fuel of its own: it just makes a spark at the tip, and the stove's gas does the burning. That's why it never needs refilling. If it stops sparking, the crystal has worn out or the wire inside has broken.

## How a Zippo Works

A Zippo uses no pressurized gas at all. The insert is packed with cotton soaked in liquid lighter fluid, and a wick runs from the cotton up into the chimney. The fluid climbs the wick by capillary action and evaporates at the top. When you roll the wheel against the flint, the sparks light that vapour. The holes in the chimney shelter the flame from wind, and closing the lid starves it of oxygen. We cover the safety side in [can a Zippo explode?](/can-a-zippo-explode/) and the upkeep in our [Zippo care guide](/zippo-repairs-how-to-take-care-of-your-zippo-lighter-and-find-replacement-parts/).

## How an Arc Lighter Works

An [arc lighter](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=electric+arc+lighter+rechargeable&tag=lightertorch-20) has no fuel and no flame. A rechargeable lithium battery feeds a small circuit that boosts the voltage high enough to jump a gap between two electrodes. The resulting arc is hot enough to light a wick or tinder the moment it touches. With no flame to blow out, it's windproof, but it only lights what you can bring into the arc. ## The Main Lighter Types Compared

| Type | Fuel | What makes the spark | Refillable? | What usually fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Disposable (BIC-style) | Isobutane | Flint and wheel | No | Runs out of fuel |
| Push-button (piezo) | Butane | Piezo crystal | Usually | Igniter wire breaks |
| Torch / jet | Butane | Piezo crystal | Yes | Jet clogs on cheap fuel |
| Zippo | Liquid lighter fluid | Flint and wheel | Yes | Fluid evaporates between uses |
| Arc | None (battery) | Electric arc | Recharge it | Battery ages out |
| Kitchen spark lighter | None | Piezo crystal | Nothing to refill | Crystal or wire wears out |

To refill any of the butane types, see [how to fill a butane lighter](/filling-up-a-gas-lighter/).

## FAQ

**What is the flint in a lighter made of?** Ferrocerium, a man-made alloy of iron and rare-earth metals, mainly cerium. It isn't the flint stone of flintlock guns. Scraping it exposes cerium, which ignites at a low temperature and throws hot sparks.

**What gas is in a lighter?** Butane-family fuels, stored as a liquid under pressure. BIC fills its pocket lighters with essentially pure isobutane. Refill cans for refillable lighters are sold as butane, which may be a blend.

**How does a lighter make a flame without a flint?** With a piezo igniter or a battery. A piezo lighter's hammer strikes a crystal that produces a high-voltage spark; an arc lighter uses a battery and a circuit to make an electric arc.

**Can you make a BIC lighter flame bigger?** No. BIC says all of its lighters are designed with a fixed flame that can't be adjusted. If you want an adjustable flame, you need a refillable lighter with an adjuster wheel.

**Why is a new lighter so hard to flick?** The child guard. US rules require disposable lighters to resist operation by young children, so the wheel or button needs extra force. It gets easier as you learn the motion.

**Why do lighters stop working in the cold?** The fuel has to boil to become gas. Near or below freezing, butane barely vaporizes, so there's too little gas to light. Warm the lighter in your hand for a minute.

**Does a piezo lighter ever wear out?** Eventually, but it takes a long time. Piezo igniters usually keep sparking for tens of thousands of presses. A dead one is more often a broken wire than a worn crystal.

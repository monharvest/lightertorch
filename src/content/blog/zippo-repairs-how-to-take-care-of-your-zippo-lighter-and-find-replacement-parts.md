**Quick answer:** Almost every "broken" Zippo is one of four things — out of fuel, a worn [flint](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=zippo+flints&tag=lightertorch-20), a charred [wick](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=zippo+wick&tag=lightertorch-20), or a dry felt pad. All four are user-serviceable in under five minutes with parts that cost a couple of dollars. If the *case* is the problem — a sprung hinge, a dead cam, a lid that won't stay shut — don't touch it. Zippo's lifetime guarantee covers mechanical failure with no charge for the repair, and opening the case yourself is the one thing that can turn a free fix into a paperweight.

## First: Diagnose Before You Disassemble

The single most common mistake is replacing the wrong part. Zippo fuel is naphtha, and naphtha evaporates whether you use the lighter or not — **a full Zippo runs dry in roughly one to two weeks sitting in a drawer**. That means "my Zippo stopped working" is a fuel problem far more often than a hardware problem.

Work through it in this order:

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Wheel sparks, no flame | Out of fuel | Refill the felt pad |
| No spark at all, wheel spins freely | Flint worn out | Replace flint |
| Weak sparks, gritty wheel | Flint dust packed in the wheel | Clean wheel, replace flint |
| Lights but flame is tiny/yellow | Wick charred or sunk too low | Trim and raise the wick |
| Burns for a second then dies | Not enough fuel absorbed, or overfilled and flooding | Refill correctly, wait 2 min |
| Lid won't stay open or closed | Hinge or cam wear | **Send it to Zippo** |
| Case cracked or hinge pin loose | Structural | **Send it to Zippo** |

## The Three Repairs You Should Do Yourself

Everything below happens to the **insert** — the metal chassis that lifts out of the case. Pull it straight up and out. Nothing you do to the insert affects your guarantee, because inserts are consumable parts.

### 1. Refuelling (the fix for most "dead" Zippos)

Lift the insert out and flip it over. Under the felt pad is packed cotton batting. Peel the pad back — a fingernail is enough — and soak the cotton with [Zippo lighter fluid](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=zippo+lighter+fluid&tag=lightertorch-20) or any naphtha-based fluid until it's damp but not dripping. Roughly a 10–15 second squeeze on a fresh can.

Two things people get wrong here:

- **Don't overfill.** Excess naphtha runs out onto your palm and then you're holding a burning hand. If fluid pools or the insert feels wet on the outside, blot it and wait.
- **Wait about two minutes** before striking, and wipe your hands. This isn't fussiness — see [can a Zippo explode?](/can-a-zippo-explode/) for what actually goes wrong with fuel-soaked lighters.

Never put butane in a standard Zippo insert. Butane needs Zippo's own pressurised butane insert, which is a different product entirely. If you want a butane flame in a Zippo case, buy the insert — don't improvise.

### 2. Flint replacement

The flint is a ~2 mm ferrocerium rod that shaves off sparks each time the wheel turns. It's the fastest-wearing part in the lighter; a regular smoker goes through one every two to four weeks.

1. Lift out the insert and find the **flint spring** — the brass screw at the bottom, usually with a flathead slot.
2. Unscrew it fully and set it aside. Do this over a table; the spring likes to launch itself.
3. Tip out the stub of the old flint. If it's jammed, a paperclip or a tap on the table clears it.
4. Drop in one new flint, thread the spring back in, and tighten snug — not hard. Overtightening strips the brass.

Zippo ships six flints per card and they fit every standard insert. Buy them in a [multipack](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=zippo+flints&tag=lightertorch-20); you'll use them.

**If sparks are weak rather than absent,** the wheel is clogged with flint dust before it's worn out. Roll it against a stiff brush or an old toothbrush to clear the knurling, then fit a fresh flint.

### 3. Wick maintenance

A wick doesn't usually need replacing — it needs raising. Every lighting chars the exposed tip, and after a few dozen strikes the burnt section is all that's left above the chimney. The flame goes small, yellow and sooty.

Grab the wick with tweezers or needle-nose pliers and pull it up a few millimetres, then snip the blackened tip flush with the top of the chimney with scissors. There's a surprising amount of wick coiled down inside — most Zippos have years of pulling-up left in them.

When you genuinely run out, replace it: pull the old wick out from the top, then thread the [new wick](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=zippo+wick&tag=lightertorch-20) down through the chimney and lay it in a zigzag through the cotton batting, not straight down. The zigzag is what keeps it in contact with fuel-soaked cotton along its whole length. Repack the cotton, refit the felt pad, refuel.

## What to Send Back — and Why It's Free

Zippo's guarantee is the real reason to own one: **"It works or we fix it free."** It has no time limit, no receipt requirement, and no registration. It covers mechanical function — hinge, cam, spring, chimney, case.

What it does **not** cover is appearance. Plating wear, scratches, a faded engraving, a chipped enamel decoration — those are cosmetic and Zippo won't restore them. Expect your lighter back working perfectly and looking exactly as beaten-up as when you sent it.

Two practical notes people learn the hard way:

- **You pay shipping to them; they pay shipping back.** The repair itself costs nothing.
- **Send the case, not just the insert**, and don't send fuel — ship it empty. Carriers treat a fuelled lighter as a hazardous material.
- If the exact part is discontinued, Zippo may return a **replacement lighter** of similar style rather than your original. If your lighter has sentimental or collector value, say so clearly in the note you include, or don't send it at all.

Start at zippo.com's repair clinic page for the current form and shipping address (the plant is in Bradford, Pennsylvania). Requirements change; the page is authoritative and this article isn't.

## Where to Get Genuine Parts

**Consumables — flints, wicks, fluid, cotton, felt pads.** These are stocked everywhere. [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=zippo+flints+wicks+fluid&tag=lightertorch-20), tobacconists, hardware stores, and zippo.com all carry them, and there's no real reason to pay a premium. Genuine Zippo flints are cheap enough that third-party alternatives aren't worth the risk of a soft flint chewing up your wheel.

**Inserts.** A complete replacement insert is the shortcut when a lighter has been abused and you'd rather not fiddle. Standard inserts, butane single-torch, and butane double-torch inserts all drop into the same case.

**Case parts — hinges, cams, springs.** Zippo does not sell these to consumers. This is deliberate: it's what the free repair service exists for. Anyone selling you a "genuine Zippo hinge pin" is selling salvage. Send the lighter in instead.

**Vintage and discontinued cases.** eBay and collector forums are the market here. Date codes stamped on the bottom of the case tell you the year, and prices for genuine vintage pieces are set by condition and rarity — a market with plenty of reproductions in it. Buy from sellers with a long feedback history and clear photographs of the base stamp.

## Keeping It Working

- **Carry it, don't store it.** A Zippo used weekly stays in better shape than one in a drawer, where the fuel evaporates and the felt dries and hardens.
- **Refuel before it's fully dry.** Running a bone-dry wick chars it faster.
- **Wipe the case with a soft cloth only.** No solvents, no metal polish on painted or enamel finishes — that's exactly the damage the guarantee won't fix.
- **Keep it out of hot cars.** Heat drives evaporation and pressurises the fumes inside the case.
- **Replace flints on a schedule** rather than waiting for failure. It costs pennies and saves you the "wheel spins, nothing happens" moment.

## FAQ

**Is Zippo's lifetime guarantee actually free?** Yes, for the repair itself. Zippo charges nothing to fix a mechanical fault regardless of the lighter's age or where you bought it. You cover shipping to them.

**Does the guarantee cover the finish?** No. Plating, paint, enamel and engraving are cosmetic and excluded. Only mechanical function is guaranteed.

**How often should I replace the flint?** Daily use, roughly every two to four weeks. Occasional use, once or twice a year. It's the cheapest part in the lighter — replace it early rather than late.

**Why does my Zippo keep going out?** Almost always fuel. Naphtha evaporates in one to two weeks even unused. If it's freshly filled and still dying, the wick has sunk below the chimney or is charred through — pull it up and trim it.

**Can I put butane in a regular Zippo?** No. Standard inserts are designed for naphtha and an absorbent cotton reservoir. Butane requires Zippo's dedicated pressurised insert, which swaps straight into the same case.

**Will Zippo return my original lighter?** Usually. If a needed part is discontinued they may substitute a comparable lighter instead, so don't ship anything irreplaceable without saying so.

**Can I open the case to fix the hinge myself?** You can, and it's the fastest way to ruin a repairable lighter. The hinge is riveted; a botched attempt is one of the few things that turns a free repair into a scrap case.
